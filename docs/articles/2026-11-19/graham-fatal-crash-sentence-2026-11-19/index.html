Allen Graham convicted in fatal Interstate 10 crash, receives maximum sentence

A Kendall County jury found Allen Remard Graham Jr., 28, of San Antonio, guilty of intoxication manslaughter, manslaughter, and intoxication assault in the head-on collision on February 16, 2025, on Interstate 10 near Comfort that killed Manuela Rodriguez, per a Kendall County Criminal District Attorney's Office release issued November 16.

Following the verdict, Graham was sentenced to 20 years in the Texas Department of Criminal Justice for intoxication manslaughter and 10 years for intoxication assault, the maximum sentences allowed by law, per the release. He must serve one-half of the sentence before he is eligible for parole.

The evidence at trial established that Graham was traveling eastbound on Interstate 10 in a Ford F-150 when he crossed the grassy median and entered the westbound lanes, per the release. He collided head-on with a Toyota Corolla driven by Rodriguez, a registered nurse who was traveling to work at Peterson Regional Medical Center in Kerrville. Rodriguez was pronounced dead at the scene, and a passenger in Graham's vehicle suffered serious bodily injury, per the release.

The investigation was led by Texas Department of Public Safety Trooper Jarrett Mieure. During a search of Graham at his arrest, Trooper Mieure located methamphetamine in Graham's pocket, per the release. Additional narcotics, controlled substances, and paraphernalia were discovered in and around Graham's vehicle, including methamphetamine, morphine, tramadol, ketamine, and syringes, per the release.

The case was prosecuted by Assistant Criminal District Attorneys Derly Chapa and Alessandra Deike.

"Manuela Rodriguez was a dedicated nurse who was simply driving to work to care for others when her life was tragically cut short. While no sentence can ever undo the loss suffered by her family, friends, and coworkers, we hope this outcome provides some measure of justice and serves as a reminder of the tragic consequences that can result when someone chooses to drive while impaired," Criminal District Attorney Nicole Bishop said in the release.
