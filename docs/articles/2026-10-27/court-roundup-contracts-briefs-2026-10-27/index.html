Court roundup: contracts approved, several items get no action

Reported by The Kendall Wire • October 27, 2026

The Kendall County Commissioners Court approved a slate of contracts and took no action on several items at its regular meeting Tuesday, October 27. Morning votes were 5-0 with all five members present. Afternoon votes were 4-0 after Commissioner Chad Carpenter left for the day.

The court approved a one-year agreement with up to two annual renewals with Recon InfoSec, Inc. for Managed Security Operations and Advanced Email Protection services, using DIR Cooperative Contract DIR-CPO-4890. The motion was made by Commissioner Richard Chapman and seconded by Commissioner Andra Wisian. No dollar figure for the agreement was stated on the record.

The court approved an agreement with Govolution LLC for electronic payment processing services for various county departments. The prior vendor gave 30 days notice that it is leaving the business, and departments must be up by November 5. The convenience fee is 2.35 percent or a $2 minimum, paid by the card user, and the county pays nothing for the service itself. The motion was made by Commissioner Jennifer McCall and seconded by Commissioner Wisian.

The court approved the first amendment to the agreement between Kendall County and the Kendall Amateur Radio Society, revising the payment the society pays the county for Tower Street radio tower improvements and repairs from $12,000 to $4,197, due to a reduction in overall cost. The motion was made by Commissioner Chapman and seconded by County Judge Shane Stolarczyk.

The court approved a sublease agreement allowing the Kendall County Youth Agriculture and Equestrian Center to sublease portions of its leased premises to the Texas Department of Public Safety for use as a CDL testing site. The motion was made by Commissioner Wisian and seconded by Judge Stolarczyk.

The court approved a license agreement with the City of Boerne allowing the city to use grounds near the courthouse, historic jail, and historic courthouse plus parking areas for the November 2026 Dickens on Main event. The motion was made by Judge Stolarczyk and seconded by Commissioner Chapman.

The court accepted a written application form for requests to establish a cemetery, under Texas Health and Safety Code Section 711.008. The vote approves the application form only, not any cemetery. The court also approved an amendment to the Kendall County Development Rules adding Section 5.12, Minimum Infrastructure Standards for Cemeteries. Both motions were made by Commissioner Chapman and seconded by Judge Stolarczyk.

The court approved the investment policy for fiscal year 2027 under Order O-20261027-2. The motion was amended on the floor from fiscal year 2026 to fiscal year 2027. The motion was made by Judge Stolarczyk and seconded by Commissioner Chapman.

The court approved the consent agenda as amended, with changes made by the county auditor to the September fee office reports. The motion was made by Commissioner Chapman and seconded by Commissioner Carpenter.

The court approved the official bonds for appointed Justice of the Peace Precinct 4 Brian Vaughn under Local Government Code 88.01. Vaughn was then sworn in as Justice of the Peace Precinct 4. The motion was made by Judge Stolarczyk and seconded by Commissioner Chapman.

The court took no action on the organizational chart. A motion to approve the updated chart, with the Alamo Area Council of Governments and the Mental Health Coalition added, was withdrawn after discussion of further additions. Members were asked to email general counsel individually rather than each other.

The court discussed revised IT Department job descriptions, including exempt-status questions and a correction on a license requirement, and pushed the item to the next court. No vote was taken.

The court took no action on the Space Needs Assessment Steering Committee. Commissioners Wisian and Chapman will work out a roster with interested parties and hold a call with HDR Architecture to clarify the scope of work. The assessment was originally budgeted at $300,000 and cut to $100,000 during budgeting.

The court took no vote on the 714 High Street setback relief request in Comfort. The owner will return in two weeks with a plat showing distances and deed language barring further encumbrance.

The burn ban stays off. The item was a standing fire-conditions update only, not a ban action. The judge reported a burn index of 557 and all seven fire chiefs unanimously recommending keeping the ban off, with heavy precipitation expected beginning Thursday. No ban order was adopted, lifted, or extended.
