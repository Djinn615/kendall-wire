Boerne council to hold hearing on Johns Road annexation, vote on stormwater charge Tuesday

The Boerne City Council meets Tuesday, November 10, 2026, at 6:00 p.m. in the Ronald C. Bowman City Council Chambers, 447 N Main St, Boerne. The posted agenda, certified November 4 by City Secretary Lori Carroll, carries a public hearing and first reading on annexing about 6.46 acres at 727A Johns Road and rezoning the tract for a planned unit development; a one-time reading of amendments to the Unified Development Code; second reading of the ordinance that would levy a monthly stormwater drainage utility charge; and a contract award for a water treatment plant carbon project.

The annexation item opens the regular agenda. Ordinance No. 2026-20 would annex about 6.46 acres at 727A Johns Road (KAD No. 14393) into the city. Ordinance No. 2026-21 would rezone the same tract from HOL interim holding zoning to R3-D-PUD planned unit development zoning, following a recommendation from the Planning and Zoning Commission. The council set the hearing for November 10 by resolution on October 27. The council also plans an executive session before the hearing to consult with the city attorney on the 727A Johns Road property.

Also in regular business, Ordinance No. 2026-22 would amend the Unified Development Code, the city's land development rules, covering general provisions, procedures, zoning, residential and nonresidential sites, environmental design, definitions, and dark sky regulations. The agenda lists it for a one-time reading, as permitted by the city charter. Ordinance No. 2026-19, on second reading, would restate the municipal stormwater drainage utility system and levy a monthly stormwater drainage utility charge on benefited property. The council held a public hearing and gave first reading on October 27; the charge would rise from $4 to $8 per equivalent resident unit.

The consent agenda, the batch of routine items the council can approve in a single vote, includes the minutes of the October 27 regular meeting and the October 29 special called meeting on the State of the City; second reading of Ordinance No. 2026-18, which would alter the prima facie speed limits on a portion of Upper Balcones Road, with a penalty of up to $200 per violation; a lease with Dell Financial Services for an amount not to exceed $204,771.50; a lease with AT&T Capital Services for iPads for an amount not to exceed $81,823.32; an interlocal agreement with Kendall County for fire services; and an amended interlocal agreement with Kendall County for library system services.

Resolutions before the council include Resolution No. 2026-R64, authorizing an enterprise asset management software agreement through the Vertosoft cooperative purchasing program, with first-year spending not to exceed $297,170.77 and pricing set for five years; Resolution No. 2026-R65, awarding the contract for construction of the water treatment plant granular activated carbon addition project, with the contractor and total cost to be named at the meeting; Resolution No. 2026-R66, setting the city's 2027 annual water commitment from the Guadalupe-Blanco River Authority at 2,205 acre feet as part of the Western Canyon Regional Water Supply Project; and Resolution No. 2026-R67, setting the city's 2027 quarterly groundwater allocation under its Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District permit at 15, 35, 35, and 15 percent.

Five shared services agreements are also on the agenda, for funding of public purpose with the Rainbow Senior Center, Friends of the Cibolo Wilderness (Cibolo Center for Conservation), Boerne Hill Country Family Services, Das GreenHaus, and Hill Country Court Appointed Special Advocates (Hill Country CASA).

The city manager's report includes an update on Dickens on Main and an update from the Gateway/Urban Design Feature Subcommittee. The council also recognizes the 2026 Citizens Fire Academy graduates and closes with an executive session on personnel matters related to the city manager's evaluation.
