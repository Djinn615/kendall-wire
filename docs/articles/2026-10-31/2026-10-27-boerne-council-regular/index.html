Boerne council adopts IH-10 dealership package on second reading, advances stormwater fee increase

Reported by The Kendall Wire • October 31, 2026

The Boerne City Council adopted the IH-10 dealership package on second reading and gave first-reading approval to doubling the city's stormwater charge at its October 27 meeting. Every announced vote was 4-0, with one member absent; the transcript does not clearly name the absent member.

The dealership package passed on the consent agenda, the batch of routine items the council can approve in a single vote (motion by Joseph Macaluso, second by Ty Wolosin). It completes the three linked items first approved October 13 for a new automobile dealership on about 13.4 acres in the 31300-31400 block of IH-10, owned by Tusculum II LLC: annexation into the city (Ordinance No. 2026-15), rezoning from interim holding zoning to C3 Community Commercial (Ordinance No. 2026-16), and a special use permit, an extra approval the city requires for certain uses, for automobile sales with accessory service, contingent on the rezoning (Ordinance No. 2026-17). The consent agenda also set a public hearing for November 10 at 6:00 p.m. on a proposed annexation of about 6.46 acres at 727A Johns Road (Resolution No. 2026-R56).

The stormwater item drew the longest discussion. The council held a public hearing, opened at 6:25 p.m. and closed at 6:28 p.m., and gave first-reading approval to Ordinance No. 2026-19, which would restate the municipal stormwater drainage utility system and raise the monthly charge from $4 to $8 per equivalent resident unit, a unit equal to 4,700 square feet of impervious cover such as roofs and pavement (motion by Wolosin, second by Bret Bunker, 4-0). Staff said the utility serves about 7,000 customers, about 6,300 residential and 700 commercial, and brings in about $670,000 a year at the current rate. The March 2022 drainage master plan identified 16 major projects costing about $60 million in 2022 dollars; the Adler Road culverts, a $5.7 million project, are complete, and the Minger Creek Bridge, estimated at about $15 million, is 90 percent designed with a federal grant application pending. One speaker, Shelly McCoy, asked whether the charge is equitable for everyone and whether it applies citywide; council members said the fee applies to everyone with no exemptions and is tiered so properties with more impervious cover pay more, and staff offered to meet with her one-on-one to answer her questions in depth. A second reading is set for November 10.

The council also gave first-reading approval to cutting the speed limit on the city portion of Upper Balcones Road from 45 to 35 miles per hour (Ordinance No. 2026-18; motion by Macaluso, 4-0). Staff said the city maintains about 0.6 miles, from the IH-10 frontage road to just past Noll Springs; the narrow road, typically 22 to 24 feet wide, saw 10 crashes on the city portion in five years, two of them serious. Traffic counts showed about 3,800 cars per day near Starbucks and Coffin Road and about 2,100 per day past Noll Springs. New signs will go in before Boerne police enforce the lower limit, and staff will look at a TxDOT safety grant in December for possible blinking lights.

In other action, the council approved the city's 2026 tax levy roll, the official roll of property taxes to be collected (Resolution No. 2026-R57; motion by Bunker, second by Macaluso, 4-0). Staff said the difference between the certified roll and the levy roll was about $6,000, not enough to require a budget amendment. The council authorized a multiple use agreement with the Texas Department of Transportation for a shared use path, an 8 to 10 foot trail, along Frederick Creek in IH-10 right-of-way, connecting the Bluff View apartments to the sidewalk system on West San Antonio (Resolution No. 2026-R58; motion by Bunker, second by Wolosin, 4-0). The developer paid $273,000 last year toward the path's design and construction; the city will maintain it, TxDOT can require its removal, and a contractor will come back to council, probably in early 2027. The council ratified a $56,092.50 replacement HVAC condensing unit for the Boerne Fire Department from Southland Industries through BuyBoard contract No. 720-23 (Resolution No. 2026-R59; motion by Wolosin, 4-0). The 18-year-old unit failed in March as an emergency purchase and was installed at the end of September.

In public comment, Trey Hargis of 115 Chama Drive told the council that Pulte Homes has applied to build a standalone sewer plant in the Corley Farms subdivision, 150 feet from his water well and farmhouse, on land he said was presented as green space and recreation. He said sludge would be trucked to Austin, potentially weekly; the application referenced a larger development of more than 700 acres and over 600 homes; and Corley Farms already has city sewer connections. He asked for a thorough, transparent review. The council took no action on the application at this meeting. Edward Rogers also presented the Texas Silver Haired Legislature biennial legislative report, covering 61 issues, on behalf of Kendall County residents age 65 and older.

In the city manager's report, Assistant City Manager Christie Stark, filling in for City Manager Ben Thatcher, said the Plant Avenue parking lot has opened but is not complete after the city ended its contractor's contract over contractual issues, with city crews to finish the work; the Main Street ramp project drew no bidders on rebid and will be rebid in January and spring. The council adjourned at 6:51 p.m. The next regular meeting is Tuesday, November 10, at 6:00 p.m.
