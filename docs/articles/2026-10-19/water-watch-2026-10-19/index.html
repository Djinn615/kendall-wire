Water Watch: Kendall County water status, October 2026

The Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District has Kendall County at stage 2 drought restrictions, the designation it has carried since the board eased back from Stage 3 at its July 13 meeting on improving groundwater levels, recent rainfall, and stronger creek and river flow. The district reviews the stage at every monthly board meeting, so each month can bring a change. Inside Boerne, city utility customers remain under stage 2 water restrictions. Lawn irrigation is limited to one day a week on an assigned day by street address, with watering hours of 7 to 11 in the morning and 7 to 11 in the evening on that day. No watering is allowed on weekends. Hand watering with a hose or bucket is permitted any time. Violations carry fines of up to 1,000 dollars per occurrence. The national picture is calmer than the rules suggest. The U.S. Drought Monitor reports 0 Kendall County residents affected by drought, placing the county in no drought category at all (data valid 10/13/26). September, however, was the fifth driest September in 132 years of records. Surface conditions and groundwater respond on different clocks, and the groundwater stages lag well behind the rain. Category detail: D0: 0 percent. Canyon Lake, Boerne's regional surface-water anchor through the Guadalupe-Blanco River Authority, stands at 907.66 feet above mean sea level, 97.1 percent full, within a few feet of its full conservation pool of 909 feet. Boerne committed to 2,165 acre-feet of GBRA water for calendar year 2026, a figure the city council set last November.
