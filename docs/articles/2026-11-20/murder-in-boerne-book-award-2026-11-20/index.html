Boerne authors take first place at state genealogical awards for Bickel massacre book

Reported by The Kendall Wire • November 20, 2026

Two Boerne authors have won first place in the Texas State Genealogical Society's 2026 Book Awards for their account of an 1868 Kendall County murder case.

"Murder in Boerne, Texas: The Untold Story of the Bickel Family Massacre," by Richard G. Tomlinson and Kathryn L. Adam-Hurst, took first place in the Books by Non-Professional, References category. The award was presented November 7 in Fort Worth.

The book reconstructs the 1868 killings of John Bickel, his 18-year-old daughter, farmhand Henry Spangenberg, and an unidentified two-year-old boy, all within four miles of Boerne. The case that followed was as tangled as the crime: six lynchings, four trials, two convictions, two acquittals, and the arrest by the Army of six Boerne citizens and the Kendall County sheriff of the day.

Tomlinson and Adam-Hurst spent three years in courthouse records and other primary sources. In the end, they write, they could not prove beyond a doubt who was behind the murders, but they believe they know, and they lay out the evidence for readers to judge for themselves.

The contest judges praised the book as a demonstration of how reasonably exhaustive research can uncover hidden truth, and called it a great teaching tool.

The Genealogical Society of Kendall County also placed at the same awards. Its journal, "Keys to the Past," edited by Adam-Hurst, took second place, according to the Boerne Star.

A copy of the book is held in the Genealogical Society of Kendall County's library collection.
