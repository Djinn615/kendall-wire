Boerne's first true public school opened 149 years ago

Reported by The Kendall Wire • November 1, 2026

On November 1, 1877, the public free school of Boerne commenced at the schoolhouse, 149 years ago today.

Boerne's early German settlers valued education, but the first teaching in town was private. Herman William Topperwein taught students at his home in 1857 at 25 dollars per month. Local histories call him the first public school teacher in Boerne, but that label is wrong: his lessons were private schooling, not public schooling.

Texas tried and failed for years to build a public system. An 1854 law organized common schools and a state school board and set aside 2 million dollars from land sales, but it provided no ongoing funding or tax support. In 1858, New Braunfels became the first town in Texas where residents voted for a tax to support a free school. In 1871, Governor Edmund Davis signed a bill starting a universal, statewide, tax-supported public education system, called a free school system. Opposition was fierce, including the charge that free schools were a Yankee idea.

Boerne did not embrace the free school idea at once. In 1873, the German Gesangverein donated land to the School Society for a school, and a school building was built the following year. It opened with 19 students in all grades, but it was not really a public school. A growing English population objected to lessons taught in German and sent their children to a school in nearby Balcones.

In August 1877, the Boerne School Association appointed Charles Dienger and F.W. Schweppe as teachers and set uniform tuition at 1.25 dollars per month. The Galveston Weekly Press then reported that the public free school of Boerne would commence on the first of next month at the schoolhouse. That date was November 1, 1877.

Boerne established its independent school district in 1909, per the Handbook of Texas.
