Water Watch: Kendall County water status, November 2026

The Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District keeps Kendall County at Stage 2 drought restrictions, unchanged from October, and City of Boerne utility customers remain under Stage 2 water restrictions as well.

The district's Aquifer Watch reading stood at 1188.10 on October 14, which the district reports as 10.09 feet below the October average. The district moved from Stage 3 to Stage 2 at its July 13 board meeting, on improving groundwater levels, recent rainfall, and increased streamflow in local creeks and rivers. The district reviews the stage at each monthly board meeting, so the stage can change at a future meeting.

Under Stage 2, Boerne utility customers water lawns one day a week on an assigned day by street address. The city newsflash lists the Stage 2 rules for potable and reclaimed water customers.

The U.S. Drought Monitor reports 0 Kendall County residents affected by drought, with 0 percent of the county in the abnormally dry category, on data valid October 27, 2026. That data date is newer than the October 13 date in the October edition, while the county figures themselves did not move.

Canyon Lake, Boerne's regional surface water source through the Guadalupe-Blanco River Authority, stood at 907.24 feet above mean sea level and 96.2 percent full on November 1, against a full conservation pool of 909 feet. That is down from 907.66 feet and 97.1 percent full on October 19.

What changed this month

The restriction stages did not change. Canyon Lake eased from its October 19 level, and the drought monitor refreshed its data date with no change in the county figures.
