New World screwworm found in Bandera County as infested zone returns

Reported by The Kendall Wire • November 11, 2026

New World screwworm has been detected in Bandera County, and the Texas Animal Health Commission has established a new infested zone covering Bandera and Medina counties, the Kendall County Office of Emergency Management and Fire Marshal said in a notice to residents this morning.

The detection was a horse in Bandera County, confirmed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture, according to Texas Tech Public Media. The commission's November 10 order establishes Infested Zone 31 for Bandera and Medina counties. The two counties were released from an earlier infested zone on September 16 after no additional detections during the surveillance period, and are now back under movement restrictions.

Kendall County is not in the infested zone. The zone now covers 25 Texas counties, according to the commission. Bandera County borders Kendall County to the west.

New World screwworm is a parasitic fly whose larvae infest wounds in warm-blooded animals, including livestock, pets and wildlife. The first detection in Texas was confirmed June 3 in a calf in Zavala County. The USDA has reported 126 confirmed animal infestations in Texas since June, with 63 still active.

Warm-blooded animals in an infested zone may not move out of the zone without prior authorization from the Texas Animal Health Commission.

The county's notice urges residents to inspect animals for unusual wounds, visible larvae or changes in behavior, and to report a suspected infestation right away. The county's guidance is "INSPECT, COLLECT, AND PROTECT."

To report a suspected infestation in livestock or pets, call the Texas Animal Health Commission at 800-550-8242. For wildlife, call Texas Parks and Wildlife at 512-389-4505. Residents should also notify their veterinarian.

The county's notice also points residents to the commission's interactive zone map, where they can enter an address to check whether a property falls in an established zone. Zone boundaries and guidance can change as the situation develops, the notice says.

What happens next: the commission and the USDA continue surveillance, fly trapping and sterile fly releases in the affected area as part of the eradication effort.
