Boerne's Veterans Plaza bronze was unveiled 34 years ago

Reported by The Kendall Wire • November 11, 2026

On November 11, 1992, a bronze sculpture by local sculptor Jay Hester was unveiled atop the monument at Boerne's Veterans Plaza, 34 years ago today.

The plaza on South Main Street has carried several names over the years: Public Square, Military Place, and Veterans Plaza. The original monument was provided by Henry and Graham. It was erected in the center of Military Place to honor the Kendall County servicemen who died in World War I, the war then called the War to End all Wars. At the time of its dedication, on March 6, 1923, it was known as the Graham Monument.

In the early 1990s, Veterans of Foreign Wars Post 688 and Boerne Post 228 helped lead efforts to revitalize the plaza. Hester created a bronze sculpture representing the branches of the armed services, and his work was set atop the original monument and unveiled on Veterans Day, November 11, 1992.

Veterans Day marks the November 11, 1918 armistice that ended World War I. Years after the unveiling, the City of Boerne, the VFW posts, and local citizens joined to renovate the plaza, replacing the deteriorating original limestone monument. The rededication of the plaza took place on Veterans Day, November 11, 2014.

The plaza still stands on South Main Street in Boerne, and its marker tells the story of the monument, the sculpture, and the rededication.
