Boerne's 1614 Bible was found in a high school closet

Reported by The Kendall Wire • November 3, 2026

For years, a shopworn old Bible sat unnoticed in a closet at Boerne High School, on the site where Boerne Middle School North stands today. In 1983, Catherine Schwarz, newly hired as the school's librarian, found it there. She gave it to the Boerne Area Historical Preservation Society, which displayed it and kept it from harm before eventually giving it to the city of Boerne.

What Schwarz found was a Low German Bible printed in 1614, published by Hans Stern of Lüneburg. Stern had bought the printing plates cheaply after the publisher of an earlier Low German Bible, the Wolderbible, went out of business. The book is written in Platt Deutsch, the Low German of northern Germany, at a time when most Bibles were still printed in Latin. Printing scripture in the language people spoke, rather than Latin, was still a new idea in the years after Martin Luther.

The Bible is one of only a handful of complete copies known in the world. Researcher Elisa McCune, whose graduate research centered on the book, puts it at one of seven copies. A University of North Texas digitization summary counts six known surviving Low German folio editions. Either way, Boerne's is the only one held by a public library. The others are at the British Library, the Royal Danish Library, the Newberry Library in Chicago, and two in Germany.

It is not a book for a lap. It weighs nearly 12 and a half pounds, measures 16 inches long, more than 10 inches wide, and 4 inches thick, and would have been displayed on a lectern in a church. Its journey to Texas would have meant six to eight weeks on a sailing ship, then a week on horseback from Galveston to Boerne.

The book was in rough shape when found. It was sent to Mary Frederickson in Canyon, Texas, who restored the cover and spine and repaired 20 damaged pages at the front. A case was made for it, and it went on display at the library.

Inside both covers is the name Johan Schwarting and the date 1660, nearly 200 years before German immigrants founded Boerne in 1849. No member of the Schwarting family has been found in Boerne proper, and how the book traveled from Europe to a Texas school closet is still unsolved.

The Bible has now been digitized page by page by the University of North Texas, and the full text is free to read online through the Portal to Texas History. The original is held at the Patrick Heath Public Library. It is no longer on display in the library's sunlit lobby, but it can still be viewed by appointment through the library's Memory Lab during archive hours Tuesday through Friday, and it rests in an archival container made by UNT when it is not being viewed.
