Outdoor burning banned across Kendall County for the weekend

Reported by The Kendall Wire • October 17, 2026

The Kendall County Fire Marshal has banned all outdoor burning across the county for Friday and Saturday, October 16 and 17, as high winds move through the Hill Country.

State regulations shut down outdoor burning when surface winds are forecast above 23 mph. Friday brought a National Weather Service wind advisory for the region, with gusts reaching 40 to 50 mph across South Central Texas, including Kendall County.

That means no brush piles, no burn barrels, and no open flames outdoors for the duration of the ban. The order comes directly from the Fire Marshal and applies county-wide.

Wind-driven fire is one of the fastest-moving dangers in the Hill Country, especially in dry fall conditions. A single escaped burn pile can become a wildfire in minutes when gusts top 40 mph.

Residents with questions about what the ban covers can contact the Kendall County Fire Marshal's office. The ban lifts when conditions improve and the Fire Marshal rescinds the order.
