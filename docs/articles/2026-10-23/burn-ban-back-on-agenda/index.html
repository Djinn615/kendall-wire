Court to weigh 90-day burn ban Tuesday

Kendall County Commissioners Court will consider a 90-day outdoor burning ban at its regular meeting Tuesday, October 27, at 9 a.m. The proposed order would prohibit outdoor burning in the unincorporated areas of the county, where the agenda says conditions have created a public safety hazard that burning would worsen.

The item comes from County Judge Shane Stolarczyk and Fire Marshal Brady Constantine. It is listed as Order #O-20261027-1. The burn ban has been off since October 2, per the county website and the Boerne Star.

The same agenda carries a wildfire-resilience block. Christie Wiley, director of the Hill Country Fire Coalition of Texas, Mark Novak, a senior wildfire fellow with the Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety, and Justice Jones of the International Code Council will update the court on a recent Hill Country Fire Coalition workshop and on the institute's Wildfire Prepared Home and Wildfire Prepared Neighborhood programs. The agenda says those programs may have the potential to provide homeowners insurance when the option is otherwise nonexistent.

The court will also consider options under Texas law to adopt relevant provisions of the International Wildland Urban Interface Code, a building and vegetation code for where development meets wildfire-prone land, alongside the International Fire Code.

The meeting is at 9 a.m. in the Commissioners Courtroom on the third floor of the Kendall County Courthouse, 201 East San Antonio Avenue in Boerne, and will also be live on Zoom. If the court adopts the order Tuesday, the ban would run up to 90 days.
