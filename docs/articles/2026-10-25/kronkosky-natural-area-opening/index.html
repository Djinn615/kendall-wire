New Natural Area Nears Opening on Highway 46

Reported by The Kendall Wire • October 25, 2026

Texas Parks and Wildlife is targeting late fall 2026 to open the Albert and Bessie Kronkosky State Natural Area to the public, site superintendent Stephanie Kwiatkowski said on the October 21 episode of the Building Texas Show.

The site sits along Highway 46 between Boerne and Bandera, straddling the Bandera County and Kendall County line. The episode describes a 3,814-acre site. No exact opening date has been posted, and the Texas Parks and Wildlife page for the site states it is not open and remains in the construction phase.

The distinction in the name matters for how the land will be used. A state natural area is land with unique natural, cultural, and historical features, and Texas Parks and Wildlife states that protecting and managing those features is its highest priority for such sites. Kwiatkowski described the choice as a spectrum between recreation and conservation, with natural areas sitting further toward conservation than state parks.

When it opens, the site is planned to offer 28 miles of multi-use trail for hikers, mountain bikers, joggers, and strollers, plus cabins, screened shelters, tent sites, and backcountry camping.

The land itself has a long backstory. Albert and Bessie Kronkosky began buying property in the area in 1946, and the couple willed the ranch to the state of Texas to protect it from development. Texas Parks and Wildlife accepted the donation in March 2011.

Kwiatkowski took over as superintendent in June 2026. She told the show the July 2026 flooding washed out landscaping, eroded trails, and damaged campsites and roads at the site before any public visitation. Staff used the event to observe how water moves across the site and to update emergency plans.

What happens next: Texas Parks and Wildlife has not posted an exact opening day. The Wire will report the date when the agency posts it.
