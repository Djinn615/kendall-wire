City Leaders Deliver State Of City

Reported by The Kendall Wire • October 30, 2026

Boerne Mayor Frank Ritchie, Mayor Pro Tem Ty Wolosin and City Manager Ben Thatcher gave the State of the City address October 29 at a luncheon at the Cana Ballroom in St. Peter the Apostle Catholic Church. The luncheon was sponsored by Liberty Capital Bank, the Boerne Star reported October 30.

Thatcher told the audience the daily cost of providing core municipal services dropped by 42 cents to $5.98 per person. The city recently approved a nearly $164 million budget for the 2027 fiscal year, the Star reported. The City Council formally adopted a $163.9 million budget at its September 8 meeting, according to My Boerne News.

Thatcher also addressed property taxes. He noted that the daily contributions help fund city services, and said the city receives no state revenue for those services.

Ritchie said public safety remains one of the city's top priorities.

"Public safety for us is one of our key issues that we want to make sure that we stay on top of for the city of Boerne," Ritchie said.

Wolosin highlighted a $2.6 million sidewalk grant project through the Alamo Area Metropolitan Planning Organization, where he serves as a city representative.

"This project is going to make our city much more walkable," Wolosin said.

Ritchie said the city plans to move forward with Fire Station No. 2 in Esperanza, with construction expected to begin in early 2027. He also said the city needs to expand animal services, noting the current facility is "busting at the seams" and that Boerne has an agreement with Fair Oaks Ranch.

Thatcher reported the Adler Road project was nearing 90 percent completion in its design phase. A public engagement meeting for Adler Road will be held November 18 at Curington Elementary School, 601 Adler Road.

Wolosin also discussed a citywide bridge grant assessment, under which the city is assessing its bridges to check whether they are safe and whether they need work.

Thatcher gave an update on improvements at the Patrick Heath Public Library, which partnered with the Mellon Foundation to secure grant funding. The library has received a new information desk and is expected to see new study pods and carpeting.

City leadership closed the address by announcing a biennial community satisfaction survey would be distributed before Thanksgiving. Thatcher said people do not have to be residents to take part.

What happens next: The Adler Road public engagement meeting is November 18 at Curington Elementary School. The community satisfaction survey will be distributed before Thanksgiving.
