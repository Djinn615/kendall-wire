The October 13 session of the Kendall County Commissioners Court brought a tense exchange during the public comment period, as former county grant coordinator Christina Bergmann addressed commissioners over the elimination of her position earlier this year.

Bergmann is not an outsider to county government. She is a sixth-generation Kendall County resident from a recognized founding family. Her grandparents started Bergmann Lumber Company in 1957, and she previously served as Precinct 1 County Commissioner herself. She holds a Business Administration degree from Southwest Texas State.

Bergmann told the court that county officials undermined her work and questioned her qualifications, but never put formal performance documentation in her personnel file. "There is not a single document in my personnel file from a county employee or an elected official regarding my job performance," she said. She noted that in November 2024, her reporting structure was changed from County Judge Shane Stolarczyk to the court directly, and said she faced repeated accusations of being unqualified without ever receiving a performance improvement plan.

District Attorney Nicole Bishop responded during the meeting, disputing several of Bergmann's claims. Bishop said she removed Bergmann from her office's grant applications over performance concerns. She acknowledged no formal improvement plan was issued but said that was not her responsibility: "I was not her direct supervisor." Bishop disputed the characterization of Bergmann's treatment as bullying, saying the actions were intended to improve performance, and said a report on the matter would be made public.

In other business from the same meeting, the court agreed to continue services under former EMS Director Ken Styskal's contract through October while the search for a permanent director continues. Brian Webb is serving as acting EMS director.
