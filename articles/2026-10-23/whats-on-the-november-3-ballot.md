Early voting is underway for the Nov 3, 2026 joint election in Kendall County. Here is what voters will see on the ballot, race by race, taken from the county's official sample ballot.

The top of every ballot carries the statewide ticket. Federal races: U.S. Senator, and U.S. Representative for District 21. State offices: governor, lieutenant governor, attorney general, comptroller, commissioner of the General Land Office, commissioner of agriculture, railroad commissioner, and State Representative for District 19. A full slate of judicial races follows, including the Supreme Court, the Court of Criminal Appeals, the 4th and 15th Courts of Appeals, and the 451st and 498th Judicial Districts.

Two local races are contested. For Justice of the Peace, Precinct 1: Michele Van Stavern (R) vs Adriann R. Wolfe (D). For Comfort ISD School Board Trustee, District 2: Alison E. Judkins vs Dawn Kay Lantz. Precinct-based races, including the justice of the peace and county commissioner seats, appear only on ballots in their precinct.

The sample ballot lists one candidate each for the county offices, meaning no challenger filed: County Judge Ricky Gleason (R), District Clerk Susan Jackson (R), County Clerk Denise Maxwell (R), County Treasurer Sheryl D'Spain (R), County Surveyor Wes Rexrode (R), Criminal District Attorney Nicole Short Bishop (R), District Judge of the 451st Judicial District Kirsten Barron Cohoon (R), County Commissioner Precinct 2 Andra Wisian (R), County Commissioner Precinct 4 Chad Alan Carpenter (R), Justice of the Peace Precinct 2 Dave Neighbor (R), Justice of the Peace Precinct 3 Debby S. Hudson (R), and Justice of the Peace Precinct 4 Frieda J. Pressler (R).

Comal ISD voters will also see two bond propositions. Proposition A authorizes $539,955,000 in bonds for school facilities, covering design, construction, rehabilitation, renovation, expansion, improvement, acquisition, equipment, the sites for the facilities, and school buses and vehicles. Proposition B authorizes $27,045,000 in bonds for technology, including new classroom computers and devices and the underlying systems and infrastructure. The ballot prints the statement "THIS IS A PROPERTY TAX INCREASE" under both.

How to vote: during early voting, any registered voter may cast a ballot at any of the county's six early voting locations. They are the Kendall County Courthouse Annex (221 Fawn Valley Drive, Boerne), Kronkosky Place (17 Old San Antonio Road, Boerne), City Hall Training Room (447 N. Main Street, Boerne), the Waring Fire House (11 Waring Road, Boerne), the Comfort Golden Age Center (628 Hwy 87, Comfort), and Comfort Baptist Church (100 Amber Drive, Comfort). Hours: 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. today and through Friday, Oct 23; 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Saturday, Oct 24; 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Sunday, Oct 25; and 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Monday, Oct 26 through Friday, Oct 30.

The last day to apply for a ballot by mail is Friday, Oct 23, and the application must be received by the county by close of business, not merely postmarked. Voters voting in person must present one of seven approved photo IDs, such as a Texas driver license, a Texas personal ID, or a U.S. passport, per VoteTexas.gov.

Election Day is Tuesday, Nov 3, with polls open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. The county's notice lists precinct-assigned polling places for Election Day, so voters should check their assigned location rather than assuming the early voting list applies.
